# Behemoth production conventions

- Art resource names follow `T_Sprite_분류_이름_상태` for character/monster paintings and `T_UI_분류_이름` for UI (`T_UI_Icon_이름`, `T_UI_Common_Rect_이름`). See Assets/Art/AGENTS.md. Preserve GUIDs and sprite geometry when renaming; delete unused art only after dependency and dynamic-load checks.

- All project illustration, including event art, must match the actual installed characters and backgrounds: bold drawn contours, substantial solid ink-black shadow shapes, simplified faces/materials and a clear focal object or action. Low exposure, a brown palette or a dark vignette do not establish this style. Reject semi-realistic facial rendering, finely modeled cloth/stone and uniform detail across the whole scene. Supply the actual project images as generation inputs, then compare the output beside them at popup size before installation. See Assets/Art/AGENTS.md for the event-art acceptance criteria.

- Project-wide art direction: characters AND backgrounds use clean large forms, restrained broad shading and deliberately few contour teeth, cracks, shards, rubble, tiny folds or facets. Do not turn angular ink art into jagged clutter, downsample to fake simplicity, or force every character scene into a dungeon. Selection illustrations express character appeal through newly composed scenes; concept art preserves identity, not pose. Apply this consistently to all future art; read Assets/Art/AGENTS.md and its style guide.

- Gameplay content must be designer-editable data, not branches keyed to an item name, item ID, or a one-off Power enum. Extend reusable trigger, condition, target, effect and formula primitives when a desired effect does not fit. Compose multiple functions with EquipmentData ability lists.
- Editable data is for values the designer intentionally varies and authors per case. Shared rules and derived results belong to system logic and must be calculated automatically from the authored inputs, not exposed as duplicate editable settings. Derive monster footprint from the lowest boundary of its authored hitboxes; show calculated results read-only if useful.
- Preserve the Ready artwork/weak-point simulation when extending the monster Inspector. Show the assigned Portrait directly beside or below its own assignment field as a separate image preview, so the designer can verify that the correct image is assigned. Portrait preview does not replace the Ready simulation.
- Keep PerkData focused on simple permanent stat choices. Triggered effects, conditional mechanics, spells and companions belong in inspectable equipment abilities and related data assets.
- Use TextMeshPro for all in-game text. Maintain the Korean text-key/CSV workflow when adding or changing UI and content.
- Preserve the Warrior's offensive pacing baseline and preserve existing B2/B3 monster tuning unless explicitly in scope. Tune Warrior defense by role: lacking other classes' unique mechanics, it should take a few more comparable attacks through guard and lose less health. Do not treat an old recovery number or an outdated description as the balance target. Act one should be challenging across its whole roster and encounter flow: health and fatigue management are core attractions. Tight Warrior guard use and appropriate weak-point play should still allow damage-free clears. Compare other classes at comparable progression, including pre-weak-point damage, break opportunities and post-break follow-up, not only theoretical burst damage.
- Monster attack names must work as natural skill names while communicating the actual pattern or concept (A–B quality, e.g. 연속 할퀴기). Avoid generic ownership templates such as “ㅇㅇㅇ의 견제” and literal instructions such as “앞발 두 번 할퀴기”. Names implying multiple hits, lifesteal or other mechanics must match the data. Record act-one animation direction and history for later art rollout.
- Use the supplied Slay the Spire event references directly to preserve the choice experience. Do not substitute a universal deferred penalty purchase for distinct event outcomes. Record references in event data.
- Production folders live directly under Assets: Art, Data, Editor, Prefabs, Scenes, Scripts. Prefer saved, editor-visible assets and idempotent editor preparation. Never require a manual scene-baking menu step.
- Preserve existing designer edits and staged work. Verify interactive mechanics in the live Unity runtime and record pending real-device or balance limitations honestly.

## Verification workflow

- Ordinary implementation requests authorize local interactive verification using the user's Unity Editor, screen, keyboard, and mouse as needed for the requested work; no separate desktop-use approval is required for that scoped test. Test only the changed behavior and directly affected interactions, then stop. This does not authorize unrelated actions or broader regression suites.
- Do not automatically run full DPS/balance comparisons or whole-system regression tests after ordinary changes. If broad coverage is warranted by DPS or core-system changes, explain the remaining verification need; run that additional suite in a separate background verification environment only when the user explicitly requests it. Keep unrun coverage clearly marked as pending.
- Keep heavy background verification serial: at most one suite at a time, without spawning extra Unity instances or duplicate suites to overlap pending runs. Background verification is a separate opt-in workflow, not part of routine local testing.

- Monster scale must be authored per species and stay unchanged in groups. Large-boss presentation means substantial on-screen body volume and proximity, not simply an elongated aspect ratio. Use the user supplied sphinx composition and place weak points on identifiable body parts.
- Evaluate act balance with explicit required-growth budgets, reward acquisition opportunities and declared perk/relic valuation assumptions. Use deterministic grown builds rather than repeated random-run averages; keep unvalidated assumptions visible.
- Multi-monster encounters have additional pressure beyond the sum of solo fights: staggered attack timings require more guard uptime and reduce safe player attack/charge opportunities. Value overlapping telegraphs, guard duty cycle and available offense windows explicitly when budgeting encounters/rewards/growth. Do not infer a difficulty increase/decrease from this principle alone, or assign an unvalidated fixed multiplier.

- Combat actor poses must be distinct authored images. Do not add keyed rotation, translation, squash/stretch, tweening or procedural pose motion to distinguish Idle/Ready/Attack/Guard. Static framing/padding compensation is allowed. Player held attacks require a valid Ready painting and a Unity-null-safe fallback that cannot display an empty white Image.
